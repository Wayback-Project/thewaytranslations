## Rule statement

Where it faithfully conveys the original passage, prefer clear action-oriented language to the inherited English “sin” family: **wrongdoing, wrong(s), do wrong, did wrong, wrong someone, moral failure**, or **error** where genuinely inadvertent. The translation must communicate actual harm, culpability, rebellion, guilt, poetic imagery, and textual ambiguity rather than presume that every use is merely a harmless mistake or missed target.

Translate each lexeme and construction in context. Differentiate generic wrongdoing, person labels, verbal forms, ritual **purification offerings**, source-specific inadvertence, personified powers in letters, and the proper geographic name **Sin**. No mechanical substitutions in canonical files are authorized.

## Rationale

Improve reader clarity and reduce unnecessarily stigmatizing religious jargon, including for readers who have experienced institutional religious harm. Preserve the seriousness of wrongs and the historical linguistic evidence.

## Acceptance criteria

- Review all 863 documented English occurrences in the 732 affected verses across all 66 books; rebase inventory against final authoritative EPUB.
- Check Hebrew/Aramaic for the Hebrew Bible, Greek New Testament witnesses as primary textual evidence, and Semitic cultural parallels with uncertainty clearly marked.
- Provide one approved verse rendering and note for each changed verse, with close review for ritual, contested, or theologically charged passages.
- Preserve canonical verse/anchor structure, terms in proper names, literary figures, and existing documentation standards.
- Generate website/mobile/search from approved EPUB; never hand-edit generated reader text.
- Keep familiar search terms as metadata aliases without rewriting displayed Scripture.
