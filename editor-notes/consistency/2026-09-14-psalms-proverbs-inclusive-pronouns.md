# Psalms and Proverbs inclusive human/divine pronoun update — 2026-09-14

## Scope

This release is deliberately limited to **Psalms and Proverbs**. It resolves reviewed `PRONOUN REVIEW`, `HIGH-PRIORITY UPDATE`, and unmistakable `DIVINE-REFERENT REVIEW` cases from the masculine-language audit.

The controlling rule is source-sensitive rather than mechanical:

- generic human beings are not narrowed to male-only English;
- actual male persons, male kinship, kings/royal figures, and sex-specific narrative participants remain male;
- when a masculine English pronoun unmistakably refers to YHWH/Elohim/God, the established divine name/title is repeated rather than substituting divine `they`, `she`, or `it`;
- disputed poetic or royal/Messianic referents are preserved rather than silently resolved;
- Proverbs 8 keeps Wisdom's feminine speaker distinct from YHWH in the creation sequence.

## Release totals

- Exact changed verses: **254**
- Generic-human repairs: **146**
- Divine-pronoun repairs: **103**
- Mixed human/divine repairs: **5**
- Books changed: **Psalms and Proverbs only**

## Intentionally preserved cases

- **Psalms 72:1-17** — Royal psalm: the masculine chain refers to the king/royal son and is intentionally preserved.
- **Psalms 89:20-45** — Covenant/royal chain refers to Dawid or the royal servant; masculine identity is preserved.
- **Psalms 109:6-19** — The adversary is presented as a specific male figure (including wife/widow language); human masculine references are preserved. Only unmistakably divine YHWH references elsewhere in the psalm are updated.
- **Psalms 110:5-7** — Known disputed royal/Messianic/divine referent; preserved for explicit editorial review rather than mechanically resolved.
- **Proverbs 7** — Narrative young man/husband and woman encounter; sex-specific participants remain sex-specific.
- **Proverbs 31:1-31** — Actual king/son/husband/woman kinship and narrative roles remain gendered. Only the generic poor-person chain in 31:7 is normalized.
