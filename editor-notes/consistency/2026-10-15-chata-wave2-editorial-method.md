## Translation philosophy

The ancient language is more nuanced than a simple claim that “sin means miss the mark.” Hebrew `ḥṭʾ` (חטא) has literal and moral senses; context distinguishes culpable wrongdoing, inadvertent failure and ritual `ḥaṭṭāʾt`. Hebrew `ʿavon` may be guilt/liability; `peshaʿ` may be rebellion; `šegagah` indicates inadvertence. In the Greek NT, `hamartia/hamartanō` carry wrongdoing and can depict a controlling power; `hamartōlos` can operate as a **socially imposed label**, particularly in Jesus' meals with marginalized people. `planē` is error or going astray, not synonymous with every instance of `hamartia`. **The canonical NT books are attested in Greek; no supposedly original Aramaic wording for those Greek verses is claimed here.** Jewish/Aramaic cultural contexts illuminate interpretation but do not supersede the written Greek witnesses.

House voice: **moral failure** where confessing, forgiving or acknowledging human conduct; **failed morally** for many verbal uses; **wrongdoing / wrong / wronged** when there are specific affected people, deliberately harmful conduct, or rule/power imagery. Use **purification offering** for the distinct priestly ritual category. Use **wrongdoing** for the kings' cultic abuses, not merely “mistake.” Original names, Exodus's wilderness of **Sin**, and Ezekiel's Egyptian stronghold **Sin** remain geographic names.

## Screenshots and checked examples

- **Matthew 3:6, Mark 1:4–5:** “confessing their moral failures,” “forgiveness of moral failures”; retain immersion, turning back and the historical Yarden/Yochanan forms.
- **Mark 2:16, Luke 15:1–2:** “people regarded/considered as wrongdoers,” making clear this can be the speakers' social judgment rather than an objective permanent identity.
- **2 Kings 15:18 and related royal formulas:** “wrongdoings of Yerovam … led Israel into wrongdoing,” retaining the persistent idol-cult pattern.
- **Leviticus 4:33, 5:7, Numbers 15:27:** `ḥaṭṭāʾt` ritual described as “purification offering,” while `šegagah`-related unwitting breaches become “acted wrongly unknowingly,” not a claim that the cultic sacrifice is “a moral failure.”
- **Romans 5:12 and Romans 7:13:** preserve the rhetorical description of wrongdoing entering, ruling, deceiving or bringing death; not a mere accidental lapse.
- **1 John 5:16–17:** distinguishes wrongdoing that does and does not lead to death and retains the instruction to ask for life for others, without defining a person as intrinsically sinful. `hamartanō` and `hamartia` are rendered appropriately as act/nominal wrong.
- **Numbers 8:21** corrects the preexisting “werehed” typo to “washed” while retaining the ceremonial purification reading.
- **Search QA:** A search for `sin` must not highlight or score `singing` or `business`; this is a separate web reader search defect and **must not trigger a change to Scripture text**. Search terms must match whole lexemes and approved old-vocabulary aliases only.

## Deliberately held (no alteration)

1. **Psalm 51:5** — poetic conception/moral state reading, avoid assigning moral culpability to the mother as fact.
2. **2 Corinthians 5:21** — `hamartia` in sacrificial context may be technical/typological; “made to be wrongdoing” can mislead.
3. **1 Samuel 15:18** — violent judgment against Amalekites; an editorial gloss would import unsupported narrative authority into the verse.
4. **Revelation 21:8** — Greek textual variant with/without `hamartōlois`; decide witness and note before alteration.
5. **2 Thessalonians 2:3** — `hamartias` versus `anomias` (lawlessness) textual variants; avoid silent manuscript policy switch.
6. **Amos 8:14** — `ʾašmat Shomron` could be “guilt/shame of Samaria” or a pejorative name for cultic deities; defer “shameful idol” reading.
7. **Hebrews 9:28** — `chōris hamartias` at second appearing could refer to no longer bearing/removing wrongdoing, not simply moral sinlessness.

Six occurrences of geographical **Sin** (four in Exodus/Numbers, two Egyptian stronghold instances in Ezekiel) are intentionally **NOT** listed as moral terms. The leftover whole-word `sin` in these passages is not a missed replacement.
