## Editorial posture

This is an editor-authorized Way Version interpretive rendering of Yeshua’s saying. It preserves “the way, the truth, and the life” while making the edition’s living-way/alignment reading explicit. Under the project’s Yeshua-sayings convention, Aramaic/Peshitta and Semitic context may inform interpretation, but this expanded English wording is not presented as a recoverable verbatim first-century Aramaic sentence.

## Source-language and contextual rationale

The release keeps the familiar three-part core because the surviving Greek text is explicit: `ἡ ὁδὸς`, `ἡ ἀλήθεια`, and `ἡ ζωή` naturally read as “the way, the truth, and the life.” The Greek does not place the word “only” before “way”; the restrictive force appears in the following statement about coming to the Parent. The immediate setting is also practical rather than abstract: in John 14:4–7, Ta’oma asks how the disciples can know the way to where Yeshua is going, and Yeshua answers by identifying himself with that way.

The Syriac tradition adds useful texture without replacing the Greek witness. In the Peshitta, `urḥā` is a concrete way/path/road word, `shrārā` is truth, and `ḥayyē` is life. The closing first-person form can carry “by me,” “in me,” or “through me” according to context. Syriac also does not use an English-style separate definite article, so article choices should be made from context rather than from a mechanical claim that the Syriac must mean either “a way” or “the way.”

The repeated Syriac first-person construction commonly translated “I am” functions normally as Syriac grammar; it does not by itself require a doctrine of two selves. At the same time, the audible repetition, the concrete path imagery, and the wider semantic range of Semitic words can support a contemplative or resonance reading in which the saying is heard as lived direction rather than only as a static proposition.

The added language about a path opening as the human self comes into alignment with the Cosmic Parent belongs to that interpretive layer. It is intended to make the edition’s reading visible: Yeshua is not merely pointing toward a route outside himself, but embodying a living way that can be entered and followed. The wording therefore combines a source-near preserved core with an explicitly interpretive expansion.

## Guardrails for future editors

- Do not cite the expanded clause as a word-for-word translation of either the Greek text or the Peshitta.
- Do not claim that Syriac grammar by itself turns “the way” into “a way.”
- Do not treat every possible root association or sound echo as the lexical meaning of the sentence.
- Preserve the distinction between **translation**, which selects wording from the source text, and **interpretive/resonance rendering**, which can make a broader reading explicit.
- If this verse is discussed elsewhere in project notes, keep the explanation concise enough that the translation remains primary; the linguistic discussion belongs in the audit trail rather than in ordinary reader-facing text.
