# Adversary / Slanderer Terminology Decision

## 2026-09-06 06:32 UTC — Source-sensitive replacement of Satan/devil language

- Context: The current corpus mixed the transliterated title `ha-Satan` with the conventional English `devil`. The editorial question was whether both should be flattened into one name.
- Decision: Accepted.
- Canonical rendering:
  - Hebrew `satan` / `ha-satan`, and Greek `Satanas` when it carries the same title, are rendered contextually as **adversary**, with **the Adversary** used for the personal cosmic accuser/opponent.
  - Greek `diabolos` is rendered **slanderer**, with **the Slanderer** used for the personal cosmic referent.
  - Greek `daimonion` remains **demon** and is not conflated with either title.
- Rationale:
  - Hebrew `satan` is originally a role noun meaning an adversary, opponent, or accuser; `ha-satan` means “the adversary,” not a disclosed personal birth name.
  - Greek `diabolos` has the distinct force of slanderer or false accuser.
  - Preserving the distinction prevents a later conventional label from erasing the source-language contrast. Revelation 12:9 and 20:2 are especially useful controls because both designations occur together.
- Context rule: Generic uses remain generic (for example, John 6:70, “a slanderer”); the title is capitalized only where the passage identifies the personal cosmic figure.
- Source track: Dual-witness—Hebrew/Semitic title restoration with Greek lexical control.
