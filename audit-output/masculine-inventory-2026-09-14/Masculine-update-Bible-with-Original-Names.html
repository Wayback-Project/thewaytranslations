2. Governing Way Version methodology

  • Begin with the Hebrew/Aramaic source and literary context; do not inherit male-only English merely because a traditional English translation used it.
  • Preserve meaningful grammatical gender and actual sex-specific identity. Inclusive language is not a license to erase real sons, husbands, fathers, kings, male lovers, or deliberately gendered imagery.
  • Human references that are not gender-specific in the source should not be narrowed to men in English.
  • Preserve ambiguity where the Hebrew preserves ambiguity; disputed poetic/Messianic speaker transitions are not mechanically resolved.
  • For an unmistakably divine antecedent, the established project rule is to repeat the divine name/title (YHWH, Elohim, God, etc.) rather than use he/him/his. Do not replace a divine referent with singular they, she, or it under this consistency rule.
  • Downstream web/mobile reader Scripture is generated from the approved canonical EPUB and must not be hand-edited independently.

3. Hebrew decision guide for this pass

Source formEditorial handling
אָדָם (adam)Often human/humankind/person. Do not default to modern male-only ‘man’ when the referent is humanity generally.
אֱנוֹשׁ (enosh)Human/mortal. Often well served by ‘mortal,’ ‘human being,’ or a context-appropriate generic expression.
בְּנֵי אָדָם (bene adam)Literally ‘children/sons of humanity’; in generic address, ‘human beings,’ ‘people,’ ‘mortals,’ or ‘children of humanity’ can preserve the sense without a men-only implication.
אִישׁ (ish)Can mean man, person, individual, husband, or each/one depending context. Do not neutralize automatically; do not masculinize automatically.
גֶּבֶר (gever)More strongly male-marked. Usually preserve male force unless the literary construction clearly functions generically.
בֵּן (ben)Son/descendant. Preserve actual kinship and male identity; generic wisdom-address uses require contextual review rather than blind substitution.
חָכְמָה (hokhmah, Wisdom)Feminine personification in Proverbs. Preserve Wisdom’s feminine presentation; do not confuse her ‘I/she’ voice with YHWH’s referent in adjacent creation language.

7. Proverbs 8: speaker/referent handling

Proverbs 8 must distinguish two simultaneous features: Wisdom is personified as feminine, while YHWH is the divine referent in the creation sequence. The audience in v. 4 is broad humanity, not males only. Therefore:

  • v. 4: remove the men-only address.
  • Wisdom’s she/I voice remains feminine.
  • vv. 22–31: when a masculine English pronoun unmistakably points back to YHWH, use the established divine name/title instead of leaving ‘he/his/him’ ambiguous.
  • Do not convert Wisdom to a masculine divine speaker and do not merge Wisdom and YHWH into one referent by accident.

8. Psalms: special cautions

  • Generic humanity formulas such as adam/enosh/bene adam often need people/humanity/mortals or a plural they-chain rather than man + he/him/his.
  • Royal psalms, named male speakers, and Messianic/royal poetry are not automatically neutralized.
  • Psalm 110:5–7 remains a documented disputed referent and is explicitly held for editorial review.
  • When a psalm directly names YHWH/Elohim and the next he/him/his clearly refers to YHWH/Elohim, follow the existing divine-pronoun rule to prevent reader confusion.

9. Song of Songs: special cautions

The Song is not a target for gender-neutralizing the lovers. The male beloved, Shlomo, and genuine male kinship/royal references should remain male. The meaningful review cases are mainly occupational/group labels that modern English unnecessarily encodes as male: ‘watchmen’ (3:3; 5:7) and ‘workman’ (7:1), plus any role noun whose Hebrew does not make sex material to the image. ‘Mighty men’ in 4:4 should be checked for whether ‘warriors’ better preserves the source role without adding modern male-only force.

Appendix B — Release rule to carry forward

Do not equate grammatical masculine with male-only meaning, and do not equate inclusive English with removing real gender. The controlling question is what the Hebrew referent actually is in context. Generic humanity should sound like humanity in English; actual men should remain men; Wisdom’s feminine personification should remain visible; and unmistakable divine referents should follow the project’s established divine-name/title repetition rule.
